Appliance Leak Water Damage in Alameda: What to Know

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

Our Alameda crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Do not vacuum unless the machine is rated for wet and dry use.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Document What FailedTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Inlet valves that stick open, so the dishwasher or washer keeps taking in water past the fill level.
  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • Dishwasher parts that wear out, including the door gasket, the drain hose and the seal on the pump.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Wall Bases and Trim

Water standing on a laundry or kitchen floor wicks up into baseboards and the bottom of the drywall. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. Sagging, staining and soaked insulation above the drywall are the usual result.

Hidden Mold at the Back

Hardly anyone looks at the dark, warm space behind a dishwasher or refrigerator. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Before cleanup, take photos, save the failed part and read your policy.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Duration of the leak, particularly a hidden drip that went unnoticed behind the machine.
  • Whether this was a sudden flood or slow seepage, and which machine it came from.
  • Whether mold has started and has to be removed before the area can be dried.
  • Removal of cabinets or flooring, compared with drying them in place when that is possible.
