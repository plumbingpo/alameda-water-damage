Wall and Drywall Water Damage Help in Alameda

Drywall acts like a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Firm panels wetted by clean water are frequently saved, in some cases with the baseboard taken off and small openings drilled so air can move inside the wall. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Signs to Look For

  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Trim along the floor that has puffed up, twisted or come loose
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Cool, slightly clammy drywall next to walls at normal temperature
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

The gypsum core turns soft and its paper starts to separate. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Walls You Cannot See

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Wall and Drywall Water Damage

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.
